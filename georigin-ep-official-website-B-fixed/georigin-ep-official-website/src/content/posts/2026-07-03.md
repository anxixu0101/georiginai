# Easing-Point 2.0: Exploring Spatial Interaction

## Designing a Smaller Version 2.0

We are currently working on the ID design and structural design for version 2.0.

We plan to switch to a smaller screen in order to reduce the overall size of the device. But honestly, what frustrates me is that the device has not become much smaller overall. The thickness has been reduced, but it is still not as thin as I hoped it would be. It looks like the hardware still needs more refinement.

At the same time, we have decided to add a Wi-Fi + Bluetooth combo module. This will allow us to keep pushing firmware updates and newly designed games to users even after they receive the product.

## Rethinking the Role of mmWave Radar

After we posted the previous simple hands-on video on Reddit, we received a lot of feedback. However, most people seemed especially interested in the tactile interaction of the lever switch. In a way, this made me even more anxious, because our most expensive module is actually the mmWave radar module. It accounts for about one quarter of the BOM cost, but so far, it has not made the product feel significantly more unique. That honestly made me feel very frustrated.

To look for inspiration, I paused my programming work and instead spent time walking along the seaside, again and again, thinking. I kept asking myself how we could make the experience more interesting.

Eventually, I came up with an idea that I think is pretty good, and I have already completed the demo code.

## A New Spatial Gameplay Demo

The mmWave radar can not only detect heart rate, breathing, and human presence, but it can also detect rough movement trajectories. Of course, it is nowhere near as precise as a SLAM algorithm, but it is enough to make Easing-Point feel much more distinctive.

![assemble the Easing-Point](/assets/images/17.jpg)

In the picture, the red dot represents my position. When I move around the room, the red dot moves accordingly in real time. The blue dot represents the position of a randomly generated item. When I move close to the blue dot, the item is automatically captured, completing one small gameplay experience.

It still looks a bit rough for now, so I will continue optimizing it.

## What Comes Next

This month, the animation designer I hired will officially join the project. There will definitely be no shortage of work ahead, but I believe we are building something truly interesting and different.

Yes, in a world full of homogeneous products, why not try to create something with its own unique character?

That is, in fact, the whole point of what I am trying to do.
